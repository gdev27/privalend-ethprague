01 — What is PrivaLend

Lend and borrow
without revealing your rate

PrivaLend is a lending protocol where your interest rate stays private. You publish what tokens you want to lend or borrow — but not the rate you're willing to pay or accept. That rate is locked in an encrypted envelope, opened only inside a secure matching engine, and the final loan settles on a public blockchain where everyone can verify it.

The problem with normal lending

When you post an offer to lend money on a traditional platform, everyone can see your rate. That's a problem: competitors undercut you, and you reveal your hand before a deal is made.

Your rate is visible to every competitor in the market
Bots and traders can front-run your position
You're forced to accept a pool rate with no negotiation power
How PrivaLend is different

You tell the market you want to lend 10,000 USDC — but your interest rate floor is sealed. A secure server matches you with a borrower, and the blockchain locks in the deal.

Your interest rate is encrypted on your device before it's sent anywhere
Multiple lenders can fill one borrower at a shared blended rate
The blockchain enforces your rate constraints — the matcher can't cheat

What is ECIES?

It's an encryption method. You lock your rate with a public key — only whoever holds the matching private key can read it. That private key lives only inside the secure matching engine, never on the public server.

What is a TEE?

A Trusted Execution Environment is a sealed section of a processor where code runs in complete isolation. Even the server operator can't peek inside. This is where rates are decrypted and matched.

What is a blended rate?

When multiple lenders fill one borrower, each may have a different rate floor. The borrower pays one single rate — a weighted average based on how much each lender contributed. Everyone's floor is still respected.

02 — Supply liquidity

Post a
lend intent

You're telling the market: "I have tokens and I'm willing to lend them — but only at a rate I set privately." You name your floor, encrypt it, and let the matching engine find you a borrower who can meet it.

Your rate is encrypted in the browser before it leaves your device. The server only ever sees a locked ciphertext — it cannot read your rate. Only the TEE matching engine has the key to open it, and it never exposes that information publicly.

Fill in your intent
What is a rate floor?

The minimum annual interest rate you're willing to accept. If you enter 500 bps (= 5%), you will never earn less than 5% on any matched portion of this intent. The blockchain enforces this — it's not just a suggestion.

IntentRegistry.postIntent(
  LEND, loanToken, collateralToken,
  maxAmount, rateBps,
  0,     // collateral ratio not used for lenders
  expiry
)
What happens step by step
1

Your rate is encrypted in the browser using the ECIES public key of the matching engine. The resulting ciphertext — an unreadable blob — is sent to the server along with your token preferences and amount.

2

The server holds your intent in a queue with other lenders and borrowers. It cannot read your rate — it just stores the encrypted blob.

3

At the next epoch, the server sends all intents to the TEE matching engine. Inside that secure environment, your rate is decrypted and paired with a compatible borrower. A cryptographically signed proposal is returned.

4

The signed match is registered and executed on-chain. The contract verifies that your rate floor is honored, then moves the tokens. Your money is now lent.

5

As the borrower repays, your share accumulates in a claim bucket. You can call LoanCore.withdrawClaim() at any time to pull out your earnings.

Your guarantee in plain English

The smart contract checks this exact condition before any loan is created:

blended rate paid to you ≥ your rate floor

This is not a server promise — it is code on the blockchain. If the matcher tries to give you a worse rate, the transaction will revert and nothing happens.

03 — Access liquidity

Post a
borrow intent

You're asking the market: "I need tokens and I'll pay interest for them — but only up to a rate I set privately, and only if my collateral terms are respected." You lock up collateral, seal your ceiling, and the matching engine finds lenders who can meet your terms.

You are protected on two fronts. The blockchain enforces that you never pay more than your rate ceiling, and that your collateral ratio is never set looser than what you specified. The matching engine cannot override either of these — they are Solidity invariants, not server-side rules.

Fill in your borrow intent
What is a collateral ratio?

You must lock collateral worth more than what you borrow — this protects lenders. If you borrow 5,000 USDC and set a 150% collateral ratio, you lock at least 7,500 USDC worth of collateral. If the value of your collateral falls below your ratio, lenders can partially liquidate your position to recover funds.

IntentRegistry.postIntent(
  BORROW, loanToken, collateralToken,
  amount, rateBps,        // your ceiling
  minCollateralRatioBps,  // minimum 10,000 = 100%
  expiry
)

How health is measured

The protocol uses Chainlink price feeds to value your collateral and your debt. Your health factor is the ratio between them, scaled to basis points. If it drops below your minimum, anyone can partially liquidate your position.

health = (collateralValue / debtValue) × 10000

What is an epoch?

An epoch is a matching round. The owner opens a window, the matcher registers the cryptographic fingerprint of a match plan, then the owner closes the window. Only after closing can the match actually execute. This two-step process prevents anyone from tampering with the plan at the last moment.

What does "At risk" mean?

When a borrower's health factor drops below their minimum collateral ratio — because collateral prices fell — the position becomes eligible for liquidation. Any wallet can repay part of the debt and receive the borrower's collateral at a discount (the liquidation bonus). This protects lenders from losses.

50%
Maximum liquidation per call — the "close factor". You can't liquidate more than half in one transaction, which prevents aggressive liquidators from draining a position instantly.
5%
Liquidation bonus — the discount a liquidator receives on collateral, above the fair oracle price. This makes liquidation worth doing, keeping the protocol solvent.
30 days
Default loan duration if no duration is specified at match time. After this, the owner can mark unpaid loans as defaulted.
05 — Complete lifecycle

How it
works, step by step

Six steps from posting an intent to withdrawing your earnings. Each step is either enforced by the blockchain or protected by cryptography. No single party — not the matcher, not the server, not even the owner — can break the rules.

1
You post an intent
You choose your token pair, size, and rate. The rate is encrypted in your browser using ECIES — a public-key encryption scheme where only the matching engine's private key can decrypt it. The encrypted rate and your other preferences are submitted to the Hono API server.

On the blockchain, your intent is stored by IntentRegistry and given a unique ID derived from all your parameters. This ID is deterministic — no one can fake or alter it.
IntentRegistry.postIntent(side, token,
collateralToken, amount, rateBps,
minCollateralRatioBps, expiry)
2
The server collects intents
The Hono server acts as a waiting room. It holds encrypted lend and borrow intents without being able to read any rates. Periodically — on an epoch tick — it bundles all pending intents into a batch and sends them to the Chainlink CRE workflow URL, which runs inside the TEE.
3
The TEE matches rates secretly
Inside the Trusted Execution Environment, rates are decrypted using the private KMS key. The matching engine runs a simple greedy algorithm: sort borrows by size (largest first), sort lends by rate (cheapest first), then greedily fill each borrow from available lend capacity.

For each match, it computes the blended rate — a weighted average of all lender rates, proportional to how much each lender contributed. If this blended rate would exceed the borrower's ceiling, the fill is rejected. The KMS signs every accepted proposal.
4
The epoch gate locks in the plan
Before any money moves, the match plan goes through a two-phase on-chain commitment. The owner opens an epoch. The matcher registers a keccak256 fingerprint (digest) of the exact match plan — lenders, amounts, rates, everything. The owner then closes the epoch.

Only after this can execution happen, and only for the exact plan that was fingerprinted. This prevents any last-second changes to the match.
openEpoch() →
registerMatchDigest(epochId, digest) →
finalizeEpoch(epochId)
5
The match executes on-chain
The matcher calls executeMatch. The MatchingCoordinator contract checks everything before touching any tokens: the epoch is closed, the fingerprint matches, the borrower nonce is fresh (no replays), the blended rate is within bounds for both sides.

If all checks pass: tokens flow from each lender to the borrower, collateral flows from the borrower into the LoanCore vault. The loan is now ACTIVE with all terms recorded on-chain.
MatchingCoordinator.executeMatch(
  params, lendIntentIds[],
  lenders[], amounts[]
)
6
Repayments and claims
The borrower repays through PositionManager. Each payment is automatically split among all lenders proportionally — if you provided 30% of the principal, you receive 30% of each repayment. Any rounding remainder goes to the first lender.

Lenders can call withdrawClaim at any time to pull out accumulated repayments. There's no need to wait for the full loan to be paid off.
PositionManager.repay(loanId, amount)
LoanCore.withdrawClaim(loanId)
Matching algorithm — explained plainly

The matching engine is a pure TypeScript function — no randomness, no state, always the same output for the same input.

01Sort borrows by size — largest borrower gets filled first
02Sort lends by rate — cheapest lender is used first (best for borrowers)
03For each borrow: pull from lenders until the borrow is fully filled
04Compute blended rate: Σ(each lender's amount × their rate) ÷ total principal
05If blended rate exceeds borrower's ceiling: reject the fill, restore lender capacity
Loan status — what each means
NONEInitial state — loan record exists but hasn't been activated yet
ACTIVELoan is live. Borrower owes principal + terms. Collateral is locked in LoanCore.
REPAIDBorrower repaid everything. Collateral has been returned.
LIQUIDATEDHealth fell too low. Collateral was seized and distributed. Loan is closed.
DEFAULTEDDue date passed without repayment. Owner-marked as an operational escape hatch.

The on-chain contracts and the off-chain matching engine use different rate formats. Any code connecting them must convert:

06 — Code reference

Smart contracts
& architecture

Seven Solidity contracts, each doing exactly one job. They are deployed separately and wired together by address — meaning any component can be upgraded independently without touching the others.

On-chain contracts — under contracts/src/
IR

IntentRegistry — the intent book

This is where lenders and borrowers publish their intentions. Every intent gets a unique ID derived by hashing all its parameters together — this makes the ID unforgeable. The registry tracks how much of each intent has been consumed. Only the MatchingCoordinator contract is allowed to mark an intent as filled. Lenders and borrowers can cancel their own intents at any time before they're matched. For borrow intents, the minimum collateral ratio must be at least 10,000 basis points (100%).

IntentRegistry.sol — inherits OwnerRoles — setMatchingCoordinator() owner only
MC

MatchingCoordinator — the settlement engine

This contract is the gatekeeper between off-chain matching and on-chain settlement. It runs the epoch lifecycle: open → register-digest → finalize → execute. You cannot open a new epoch while the current one is still open. When a match is executed, this contract verifies the cryptographic fingerprint, checks all rate constraints, prevents replay attacks via borrower nonce tracking, and only then calls LoanCore to move the tokens.

MatchingCoordinator.sol — inherits OwnerRoles — matcher role has registerMatchDigest and executeMatch access
LC

LoanCore — the vault

This contract holds all the money — both principal from lenders and collateral from borrowers. Only the MatchingCoordinator can create loans. Only PositionManager and LiquidationManager can update them. When a borrower repays, LoanCore splits the payment among lenders proportionally. Integer division rounding always goes to the first lender. Lenders call withdrawClaim() here to pull their accumulated share.

LoanCore.sol — inherits OwnerRoles — allowlists coordinator, positionManager, liquidationManager
PM

PositionManager — borrower controls

The interface borrowers use to manage their loan after it's created. Handles repayment, adding collateral, removing collateral, and closing a fully repaid position. Before allowing collateral removal, it checks the oracle to ensure the health factor wouldn't drop below the loan's minimum. Token decimal differences (e.g. USDC has 6 decimals, WETH has 18) are handled here using IERC20Metadata.

PositionManager.sol — inherits OwnableLite — setOracle() owner only
LQ

LiquidationManager — open to everyone

Any wallet can call liquidate() if a borrower's health factor is below their minimum. The call is capped at the close factor (default 50%) — you can't liquidate an entire position in one transaction. The liquidator repays some debt and receives collateral at oracle price plus a 5% bonus. This bonus is capped at 50% to prevent absurd configurations. The liquidation payment is distributed to lenders exactly like a normal repayment.

LiquidationManager.sol — inherits OwnableLite — closeFactor and liquidationBonus are configurable by owner
Oracle stack — pricing for health checks and liquidations
CL

ChainlinkOracle — price source

Maps each token address to a Chainlink price feed. The owner sets how stale a price can be before it's rejected. Rejects zero or negative prices and stale timestamps. Returns all prices normalized to 8 decimal places so the rest of the contracts can do consistent math.

oracle/ChainlinkOracle.sol — implements IPriceOracle
OR

OracleRouter — emergency override layer

Sits in front of ChainlinkOracle. If a Chainlink feed goes wrong, the owner can set a manual price override for any token, or pause a token's feed entirely, without redeploying anything. In normal operation, it passes all price queries straight through to ChainlinkOracle. Both PositionManager and LiquidationManager hold their own oracle reference — keep them pointing at the same OracleRouter in production.

oracle/OracleRouter.sol — inherits OwnableLite
Off-chain components
engine/ — the matcher

A pure TypeScript function. engine.ts runs the greedy matching algorithm. ecies.ts decrypts rates when the CRE private key is present. canonical.ts computes the keccak256 proposal hash so the on-chain contract can verify it. main.ts is the Chainlink CRE HTTP entry point.

server/ — the intent pool

A Hono REST server that collects intents and triggers matching rounds. It POSTs intent batches to the CRE workflow URL and stores KMS-signed proposals with a TTL. Note: executor.ts targets an older monolithic contract ABI — it needs an adapter to work with the current MatchingCoordinator.

client-snippets/ — browser encryption

A reference implementation of encryptRate() using the eciesjs library and the CRE public key. This is the first step in any intent submission — without encrypting, your rate would be transmitted in plaintext and the server could read it.

Known gaps in the current implementation

Interest doesn't accrue yet

The weightedRateBps field is stored on the loan for future use, but principal doesn't compound. The rate is metadata only — what you see is the rate at match time, not a growing balance.

Rate format must be converted at the bridge

The Solidity contracts use basis points (500 = 5%). The TypeScript engine uses plain decimals (0.05 = 5%). Any code that passes proposals from the engine to executeMatch must convert these consistently.

Intent IDs aren't linked between server and chain

On-chain, intent IDs are 32-byte hashes. The server uses UUID strings. If you post to both, you need to maintain your own mapping — store the chain ID after postIntent() returns the transaction receipt.

executor.ts needs an ABI adapter

The executor in server/src targets a legacy contract interface (settleMatch) that doesn't exist in the current codebase. The correct on-chain settlement path is MatchingCoordinator.executeMatch after epoch finalization.

07 — Privacy & trust

Who knows
what, and why it matters

PrivaLend doesn't eliminate trust — it carefully separates it. Different parts of the system know different things, and different guarantees come from different sources. Here's exactly what you're trusting and what is cryptographically or contractually enforced.

The core idea: separate what is seen from what is enforced

The public server can see your token amounts but not your rate. The TEE sees your rate but can't be watched. The blockchain sees the final terms but enforces them with code. Each layer does one job and can't do the others' jobs — that's how privacy and integrity coexist.

Layer 1 — The public API server (Hono)

Semi-trusted · can be compromised without breaking rate privacy
What the server can see
Your wallet address — necessary to route intents
Token pair and intent size — these are public by design
Collateral amounts — also public
Your encrypted rate — an unreadable ciphertext blob, useless without the TEE key
What the server cannot see
Your actual interest rate — locked behind ECIES encryption
Any other lender or borrower's rate — all are equally opaque
You must trust the server to forward intents honestly and trigger epochs on time

Layer 2 — The TEE matching engine (Chainlink CRE)

Highest privacy boundary · rates visible only inside the enclave
What the TEE can see (only inside the enclave)
Decrypted rates — temporarily, during matching only
The proposed match — before it's signed and sealed
What you gain from using a TEE
Rates never leave the enclave in plaintext — not even to the server operator
Every match proposal is KMS-signed — the blockchain can verify the signature
Only the signed result exits the TEE — not the raw inputs
You trust: that the CRE host runs unmodified code, that KMS is correctly configured, and that the binary matches the audited source

Layer 3 — The blockchain (Ethereum)

Fully public · enforces invariants in code · cannot be overridden
What becomes fully public
All intent parameters — once posted, the chain stores them permanently
All loan terms — who lent what, at what rate, with what collateral
Every repayment and liquidation event — fully auditable history
What you still trust
The matcher — to register correct digests and not censor intents
The owner — controls who can be a matcher and manages epoch timing
Chainlink oracle feeds — wrong prices can trigger wrongful liquidations
ERC-20 token contracts — standard transfer behavior is assumed throughout
